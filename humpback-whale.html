The humpback whale and the crab-trap lines

The story, step by step

Humpback whale

Meet the humpback whale.

The singer of the sea. Humpbacks can live 80 to 90 years (NOAA Fisheries). But this one has a problem.

Tangled

Scientists spot a tangled whale.

Lines from crab traps are wound around its tail. Every move is hard work, and the traps drag behind it.

Not so easy

The first try does not work.

The lines are thick and tight. The crew is tired, and the boat is running low on fuel.

Free!

The last line comes off.

The crew keeps going, and at last the whale is free. The traps sink away, and the whale swims off.

A long life

Scars, but alive.

The lines left scars on its tail. It kept swimming, singing and travelling for many more years, all the way to a ripe old age.

Gone

The whale is not there anymore.

The next day the crew comes back to the same spot. The whale has already left the bay, and nobody can find it.

A sad ending

Too tired to go on.

With the traps still on its tail, the whale could barely move. It grew weak and thin, and in the end it died. It became one more victim of fishing gear.
